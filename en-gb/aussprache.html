Improve your pronunciation with the app – for German and many other languages

Listen, repeat slowly, compare: this is how you’ll sound more natural, step by step

Are you looking for an app or software to improve your pronunciation? Then one thing matters above all else: listen carefully before you speak.

Why good pronunciation starts with listening

When you hear unfamiliar sounds, your ear first associates them with the sounds of your mother tongue. As long as you can’t hear the difference, you’ll struggle to pronounce it accurately. That’s why it’s of little use to memorise rules from an IPA chart. It’s better to listen to plenty of audio from native speakers, repeat slowly along with them and compare yourself as you go – just like a child learning their first language.

How to practise pronunciation with the app

  1. Understand first: the app decodes your text word by word – the meaning in your native language is shown beneath each word. If you know what you’re hearing, you can concentrate fully on the sound.
  2. Listen slowly: Turn the speed down. The native-speaker-quality voice remains natural, and the word just spoken is highlighted – so you can see exactly which sound belongs to which word.
  3. Practise difficult words individually: Press and hold a word for a few seconds, and the app will repeat just that word, slowly and clearly. For whole phrases, use the A-B repeat function to loop the section.
  4. Keep listening in the background: Let the text play on a continuous loop in the background afterwards – this way you’ll absorb the sound and melody without any effort.
  5. Speak along: Play a short sentence very slowly and speak along with it at the same time. You’ll hear your own voice right next to the model and can correct yourself. Once you’ve got the hang of it, increase the tempo – right up to normal speed.

These steps follow an approach inspired by the Birkenbihl Method. Our interactive explanation of the method shows you how they work together.

Foreign scripts: Transliteration as a bridge

For languages such as Arabic, Japanese, Chinese, Greek or Ukrainian, the app also displays a transcription in Latin letters beneath each word. This allows you to repeat the words straight away, even before you can read the script.

German pronunciation: common difficulties

  • Umlauts: ‘schön’ and ‘schon’ (pretty and already) or ‘Tür’ and ‘Tour’ (front door and tour) differ by just one sound – yet have different meanings.
  • The ‘ch’ sound: in ‘ich’ it sounds different to in ‘ach’.
  • Word endings: At the end of a word, ‘Tag’ ends with a k sound and ‘Hund’ with a t sound.
  • Stress: It often falls on the first syllable, but many words such as ‘Familie’ or ‘verstehen’ are stressed on the second.

The quickest way to learn these differences is to listen to the same words slowly and repeatedly, and then repeat them aloud.

From repeating after the recording to speaking freely

Once you’re familiar with the sounds, it’s time for real conversation. In a dialogue, you speak into the microphone with an AI conversation partner and hear the reply in your target language. Tip: Read short texts on topics that really interest you – that way, you’ll find it easier to keep at it.

Frequently asked questions

What is choral speaking?

You play back a short sentence very slowly and speak along with it at the same time. Because you hear your own voice right next to the original, you’ll immediately notice where you’re going off track and can correct yourself.

Do I need the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA) for this?

No. You learn the pronunciation by ear: listen, repeat slowly, compare. For languages with non-Latin scripts, the app also shows you a transcription in Latin letters.

Does this only work for German?

No. The exercises work with any text in your target language – whether it’s German, English, Spanish, Japanese or Arabic.
