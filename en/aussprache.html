Improve Your Pronunciation with the App—for German and Many Other Languages

Listen, repeat slowly, compare: Here’s how to sound more natural, step by step

Are you looking for an app or software to improve your pronunciation? Then one thing matters most: listen carefully before you speak.

Why Good Pronunciation Starts with Listening

When you hear unfamiliar sounds, your ear first associates them with the sounds of your native language. As long as you can’t hear the difference, you’ll have a hard time pronouncing it correctly. That’s why memorizing rules from an IPA chart isn’t very helpful. It’s better to listen to plenty of audio from native speakers, repeat slowly along with them, and compare yourself to them—just like a child learning their first language.

How to practice pronunciation with the app

  1. Understand first: The app decodes your text word by word—the meaning in your native language appears below each word. When you know what you’re hearing, you can focus entirely on the sound.
  2. Listen slowly: Turn down the speed. The native-speaker-quality voice remains natural, and the word just spoken is highlighted—so you can see exactly which sound belongs to which word.
  3. Practice difficult words individually: Press and hold a word, and the app will repeat only that word, slowly and clearly. For entire phrases, use the A-B repeat function to loop the section.
  4. Keep listening in the background: Let the text play on a continuous loop in the background afterward—this way, you’ll memorize the sound and melody without any effort.
  5. Chorus practice: Play a short sentence very slowly and speak along with it. You’ll hear your voice right next to the original and can correct yourself. Once you’ve got it, increase the tempo—up to normal speed.

These steps follow an approach inspired by the Birkenbihl Method. Our interactive explanation of the method shows you how they work together.

Foreign Scripts: Transliteration as a Bridge

For languages such as Arabic, Japanese, Chinese, Greek, or Ukrainian, the app also displays a transcription in Latin letters beneath each word. This allows you to repeat along immediately, even before you can read the script.

German Pronunciation: Common Difficulties

  • Umlauts: “schön” and “schon” (beautiful and already) or “Tür” and “Tour” (front door and tour) differ by only one sound—yet have different meanings.
  • The “ch” sound: It sounds different in “ich” than in “ach.”
  • Word endings: At the end of a word, “Tag” ends with a k sound and “Hund” ends with a t sound.
  • Stress: It often falls on the first syllable, but many words, such as “Familie” or “verstehen,” are stressed on the second syllable.

The fastest way to learn these differences is to listen to the same words slowly and repeatedly, and then repeat them aloud.

From Repeating to Speaking Freely

Once you’re familiar with the sounds, it’s time for real conversation. In a dialogue, you’ll speak into the microphone with an AI conversation partner and hear the response in your target language. Tip: Read short texts on topics that really interest you—that way, it’ll be easier to stay on track.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is shadowing?

You play back a short sentence very slowly and speak along with it. Because you hear your voice right next to the original, you immediately notice where you’re off and can correct yourself.

Do I need the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA) for this?

No. You’ll learn pronunciation by ear: listen, repeat slowly, and compare. For languages with non-Latin scripts, the app also shows you a transcription in Latin letters.

Does this only work for German?

No. The exercises work with any text in your target language—whether it’s German, English, Spanish, Japanese, or Arabic.
