Why B2 is crucial for your career in caregiving

Thousands of caregivers from Slovakia, Romania, and other countries work in Austria providing 24-hour care—many of them with nursing training or even a nursing diploma from their home countries. The difference between working as an independent caregiver and holding a permanent position as a nursing assistant or a certified healthcare and nursing professional (DGKP) is enormous: regular working hours, a collective bargaining agreement, 13th and 14th month salaries, paid vacation, and sick leave.

The key to making this transition is almost always the same: the recognition or nostrification of your degree—and for that, the authorities require proof of your German language proficiency. In higher-level nursing professions, this is generally level B2 of the Common European Framework of Reference for Languages. B2 means you can communicate spontaneously and fluently, even on specialized topics—exactly what you need every day during shift handoffs, in care documentation, and when speaking with doctors and family members.

In other words: Often, the only thing standing between you and a significantly better-paying, socially secure job isn’t a new degree—it’s a language certificate.

What German language level do you need for what?

  • 24-hour care (self-employed): No certificate required. You must be able to communicate in everyday situations with the person receiving care and their family members—many agencies expect a level of A2 to B1, for example.
  • Home health aide and caregiving professions with employment: Requirements are set by the federal states and employers; at least B1 is usually expected.
  • Care Assistant (PA) and Certified Care Assistant (PFA): For training or recognition of foreign qualifications, German language skills at the B1 to B2 level are usually required—the exact requirements can be obtained from the relevant authority in your federal state.
  • Certified Health and Nursing Professional (DGKP): Generally B2. Without this certification, you are not authorized to practice and will not be entered into the Health Professions Register.

Important: You should always check with the relevant authority to find out the specific requirements for your particular process—for DGKP recognition, this is the universities of applied sciences; for nursing assistant professions, it is the state government offices. How long does it take to reach B2?

As a rough guideline, language institutes estimate 600 to 800 instructional units from absolute beginner to B2. That sounds like a lot—but this figure applies to traditional classroom instruction. Anyone living and working in Austria has a huge advantage: daily exposure to the language.

Realistically speaking: With an intensive course plus daily self-study, many learners reach B2 in 12 to 18 months. Caregivers working on a rotating schedule (14 days on, 14 days off) can use their time off for focused study sessions and keep up with their learning in small daily increments during their work shifts. What matters most isn’t the number of hours, but consistency—and that you’re learning material that truly helps you progress.

Example: Here’s what your learning routine might look like on a rotating schedule

Many caregivers don’t fail because of a lack of willpower, but because of a lack of a plan. Here’s what a realistic schedule might look like:

  • During the work cycle (14 days): 20 to 30 minutes daily on your phone—decoding a short text, listening to the pronunciation, and passively listening while cooking or going for a walk. Additionally, make a point of having a conversation with the person you’re caring for every day and look up new words immediately.
  • During the 14 days off: Three to four longer study sessions per week (60 to 90 minutes)—grammar from the textbook, practicing exam questions, working through longer specialized texts. If possible, take an intensive or online course during this time.
  • One goal each month: For example, “I can summarize a handover verbally” or “I can understand a care report without a dictionary.” Small, measurable goals keep motivation high.

At this pace, you’ll accumulate 15 to 20 hours of study per month—enough to advance from B1 to B2 in just over a year without having to take time off from work.

Which Exams and Certificates Count

In Austria, three exam providers are particularly common:

  • ÖSD (Austrian Language Diploma in German): the standard Austrian exam, available at many locations both in Austria and abroad.
  • Goethe-Institut: best known internationally, with test centers in Bratislava, Bucharest, and many other cities.
  • telc: even offers its own exam with nursing-specific terminology, called “telc Deutsch B1–B2 Pflege.”

Before registering, check with your accreditation authority to see which certification is accepted for your application process—that way, you won’t end up paying for the same exam twice. Exam fees typically range from 150 to 250 euros, depending on the provider and country.

Learn faster with your own specialized texts

The most common mistake on the path to B2: spending months memorizing textbook dialogues about restaurant visits and vacation planning—and then not understanding a single word when faced with your first nursing documentation. For certification and your day-to-day work, you need technical terminology: body parts, medical conditions, medications, and handover phrases.

This is exactly where an approach inspired by the Birkenbihl Method comes in: Instead of cramming vocabulary lists, you take real texts from your everyday professional life and understand them word for word. Your brain learns the language in context—just as you learned your native language.

Courses and Financial Aid

Learning German doesn’t have to be expensive. Here are some resources you should know about:

  • AMS (Public Employment Service): Under certain conditions, it subsidizes German courses and certifications, especially if the certification leads to a job in a field with a labor shortage—and nursing is such a field.
  • ÖIF (Austrian Integration Fund): offers subsidized German courses and provides free learning materials, including job-specific resources for the care sector.
  • Chamber of Labor (AK): Depending on the state, education vouchers or grants for language courses are available.
  • Adult Education Centers (VHS): relatively affordable courses throughout Austria, often offered in the evenings and on weekends.

Tip: Almost always apply for financial aid before booking a course—reimbursements are rarely paid retroactively.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a B2 level of German for 24-hour caregiving?

No, no certificate is required for independent personal care—you just need to be able to communicate with the person you’re caring for and their family members. You’ll only need a B2 level if you want to transition into a legally regulated care profession, such as a nursing assistant or a certified nurse.

Which German language certificates are recognized in Austria?

The most common exams are those offered by ÖSD, the Goethe-Institut, and telc. You can find out directly from the relevant authority—such as the Ministry of Health or the university of applied sciences handling your application—which certificates they accept for the recognition of your care profession.

How long does it take to reach German B2 level?

Language schools generally estimate that it takes roughly 600 to 800 class hours to reach B2 starting from scratch. Those who are already working in Austria and hear German every day usually progress much faster—especially if they also study specifically using job-related texts.

Is everyday German sufficient for working in a nursing home?

To work in a legally regulated nursing profession, you’ll also need specialized terminology: care documentation, shift handoffs, and conversations with doctors and family members. That’s why it’s worth learning from the start using specialized nursing texts rather than just everyday dialogues.

Are there financial aid options for German courses in Austria?

Yes. Depending on your situation, you may be eligible for funding from the AMS, the Austrian Integration Fund (ÖIF), or the educational grants offered by your state’s Chamber of Labor. Be sure to inquire before booking a course—many grants require you to apply in advance.

Can I learn German while providing 24-hour care?

Yes—the shift schedule of 14 days on and 14 days off is particularly well-suited: During your shift, you can study daily in short sessions on your phone and practice speaking with the person you’re caring for; during your time off, you can deepen your knowledge with longer study sessions or a course.
