German for Certification as a Registered Nurse — telc B1·B2 Nursing and Technical Language Exam

What level is actually required, how the exam is structured, and what the difference in euros amounts to

As of August 2026

Nursing is by far the most common profession for which recognition is sought in Germany: In 2024, 32,500 of the total 79,100 positive recognition decisions were for registered nurses — that is 41 percent (Federal Statistical Office, 2024 data). This page explains what level of German is required for this and what causes people to fail in practice.

What level of German do I need for recognition as a certified nursing professional?

The nationwide standard is B2 according to the Common European Framework of Reference for Languages. This is based on the key points paper from the 92nd Conference of Health Ministers in June 2019. Speech therapy is an exception; a C2 level is required for that field.

A B1 level is not sufficient for recognition as a skilled nursing professional in any federal state. With a B1 certificate, you’ll only reach the level of an assistant or aide—in other words, exactly the position you’re trying to move beyond. This distinction is the most costly mistake in nursing certification, because it only becomes apparent after the exam.

In its key points paper, the Conference of Health Ministers itself states that general language certificates “are often not suitable for assessing the language skills required to practice the profession.” This is precisely why the nursing-specific professional language exam was developed, which is now required by several federal states.

Is a general B2 certificate sufficient, or do I need a specialized language exam?

That depends on the state, and there are significant differences. In Bavaria, a general B2 language certificate is sufficient; there is no separate specialized language exam for nursing care there. In Bremen, Hamburg, Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania, Schleswig-Holstein, Berlin, and Saxony-Anhalt, however, a B2 specialized language exam for nursing is required. In Rhineland-Palatinate and North Rhine-Westphalia, the exam is administered by the nursing chamber.

As of August 2026, subject to change. Responsibilities and requirements change regularly—always check with the relevant state office or nursing chamber before registering. The federal state also determines which certificates are recognized. According to its information sheet, Bavaria accepts, among others, Goethe B2, the Goethe-Test PRO Pflege, telc B2, telc Deutsch B1·B2 Pflege, TestDaF with TDN 3, ÖSD B2, ECL B2, the BAMF German Test for the Workplace B2, and the BSB Nursing Language Proficiency Exam.

Three pitfalls regarding certificates. First: telc certificates from Serbian and Bosnian institutions are no longer accepted as of June 23, 2022. Second: telc German B2·C1 Medicine is not for nursing professionals—that is the medical terminology exam; for nursing, telc German B1·B2 Nursing is the correct exam. Third: To obtain a visa under Section 16d of the Residence Act, your language certificate must be no more than 12 months old, and the provider must be ALTE-certified.

How is the telc German B1·B2 Nursing exam structured?

The exam consists of a written section lasting 115 minutes plus a 20-minute break, and an oral section with 20 minutes of preparation and approximately 16 minutes of exam time, conducted as a pair exam. All tasks are set in the context of everyday nursing practice: answering machines, team meetings, care planning, and admission reports.

In terms of preparation, this means: Everyday German alone isn’t enough. The exam tests nursing-specific terminology—documentation, handoffs, and team discussions. Those who study only from general textbooks will encounter text types in the exam that they’ve never seen before.

What happens if I only achieve a B1 level?

The telc German B1·B2 Nursing exam offers two possible certificates. If you score 75 out of 100 points or higher, you’ll receive a B2 certificate, which is sufficient for certification as a nursing professional. If you score between 35 and 74.5 points, you’ll receive a B1 certificate—which is worthless for professional recognition. Half a point thus determines your overall success on the exam.

Results are available after four to six weeks. The exam can be retaken as often as desired, but always in its entirety and at full price—there is no option to retake only the failed portion. So, anyone who scores 74.5 points will pay the full amount again for the second attempt and invest several more weeks.

What this means for preparation: The gap between B1 and B2 in this exam is not a difference in proficiency that can be made up later, but a threshold that must be cleared on a single exam day. It’s worth aiming for well over 75 points rather than just barely reaching it.

How much does the exam cost?

The telc German B1·B2 Nursing exam costs about 200 to 260 euros per attempt, depending on the test center. telc does not specify a standard list price; the centers set their own rates—one documented example is 219 euros in Hamburg. Therefore, always check the price directly with the test center before you register.

The specialized language exam should be considered separately. It costs 375 euros in Bremen, Hamburg, Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania, Schleswig-Holstein, Berlin, and Saxony-Anhalt, and 450 euros in Rhineland-Palatinate. In the states listed first, it’s free if you’re taking a BAMF-funded B2 nursing course—that’s the most effective money-saving tip on this page.

What is the step-by-step process for recognition?

The process is always handled by the federal state where you want to work—there is no federal agency for nursing recognition. From the application to the certificate, you’ll go through six steps.

Step 1: Submit your application to the state office

Submit your application to the state office of the federal state where you want to work. Parallel applications to multiple offices are not permitted—you must choose one federal state. North Rhine-Westphalia additionally requires a connection to NRW, such as an employment contract, verifiable job applications, or a business plan.

Step 2: Equivalency Assessment

Your training will be compared to the German training program for a registered nurse. You may waive the in-depth examination. This significantly shortens the process but leads you directly to the compensatory measure—without the chance that the authorities will determine full equivalence.

Step 3: Decision

The decision either establishes full equivalence, sets a requirement (known as a “deficit decision”), or results in a rejection. Across all professions, only 1 percent of procedures ended in a rejection in 2024, while 45 percent resulted in a requirement (Federal Statistical Office, 2024 data).

Step 4: Equivalency Measure—The Choice Is Yours

If a condition is imposed, you decide whether to take a knowledge test or complete an adjustment training course. This right to choose is set forth in §§ 40 ff. of the Nursing Professions Act and lies expressly with the applicant, not with the authority.

Step 5: Language Proficiency Proof

Proof of language proficiency is required at different stages of the process depending on the federal state. In Baden-Württemberg, B2 proficiency is not required until the certificate is issued, whereas in other states it is required earlier in the process. Check with your state office to determine the exact timing so that you neither wait unnecessarily nor apply too late.

Step 6: Certificate and Professional License

After successfully completing the compensatory measure and providing proof of language proficiency, you will receive the certificate or work permit. It is valid nationwide—so you are not restricted to the state in which you completed the process.

Regarding duration: The statutory deadline for the first decision is four months from the date the documents are complete. According to the BIBB (data from 2017 to 2020), the process—including the compensatory measure—takes an average of about 15 months until the final decision is issued. The IAB Research Report 22/2024 shows that in half of the cases involving additional requirements, up to 426 days elapsed between the initial notice of deficiencies and the final decision alone. If you have a degree from a third country with additional requirements, realistically plan for 12 to 24 months.

Knowledge Test or Adaptation Course—Which Is Better for Me?

The knowledge test is the faster route; the adaptation course is the safer one. The knowledge test is a single exam held on one date, in which each section may be retaken only once. The adaptation course lasts at least six months and no more than three years, but can be extended. There are no statistics on how long preparation for the knowledge exam takes on average—so do not plan on it as a fixed timeframe.

The point many people overlook: The practical skills test takes place at the workplace. It’s not possible without a cooperating employer. Clarify this before you decide to take the skills test—otherwise, you’ll choose the quick route and then find yourself without a test location.

How much more will I earn as a certified specialist?

The difference is around 600 to 760 euros gross per month and, realistically, 300 to 400 euros net. According to the TVöD, an unskilled nursing assistant in pay grade P5 earns 2,907 to 3,629 euros gross, while a certified nursing professional in P7 earns 3,510 to 4,305 euros.

This disparity is not just an individual issue. In nursing, 34 percent of foreign workers are employed at the assistant level, and in elder care, 66 percent; among German workers, the figures are 16 percent and 47 percent, respectively. The Institute for Employment Research explicitly cites the lengthy recognition processes as the reason.

What support is available?

For language training, the BAMF special course for healthcare professions (B2) is the primary option: approximately 600 instructional units, open to nursing, midwifery, and physical therapy professionals starting at level B1. Those earning no more than 20,000 euros per year or receiving benefits under SGB II or SGB III may participate free of charge; above this threshold, each instructional unit costs 2.56 euros, with a 50 percent refund upon passing the exam.

The federal government provides a recognition grant to cover procedural costs: procedural costs ranging from 100 to 600 euros, certificate evaluation and qualification analysis up to 1,200 euros, and qualification measures up to 3,000 euros. Since January 1, 2025, the income limit has been 32,000 euros for individuals and 50,000 euros for joint tax filers. Eligibility requires a primary residence in Germany and no concurrent financial assistance from the Employment Agency or Job Center.

The recognition grant does not cover language courses or language test fees. This is expressly stipulated in the regulations. So, for German, you’ll need to find another way—a vocational language course through the BAMF, an education voucher for AZAV-certified courses, the Jobcenter, or, in Hamburg, the local scholarship program. Also: The funding application must be submitted before the recognition application.

The recognition counseling offered through the IQ funding program is free and available regardless of income. It’s the best first point of contact because it clarifies which state office is responsible, what documents are needed, and in what order you must submit your funding applications.

The Most Common Mistakes

Most setbacks in nursing recognition aren’t caused by a lack of skill, but by formalities that no one explained beforehand. In practice, these twelve points cost time and money.

Language

  • A score of 74.5 on the telc B1·B2 Nursing exam results in a B1 certificate—which is worthless for nursing certification, even though the exam appears to have been “passed.”
  • A general B2 certificate is not sufficient in all federal states. Moving to another federal state may trigger additional requirements.
  • telc certificates from Serbian and Bosnian institutions have not been accepted since June 23, 2022.
  • For a visa under Section 16d of the Residence Act, the language proficiency certificate must not be more than 12 months old.

Procedure

  • Parallel applications in multiple federal states are not permitted.
  • The four-month deadline begins only once the documents are complete—not upon receipt of the application.
  • The application for financial support must be submitted before the application for recognition.
  • In North Rhine-Westphalia, the required connection to the state is often missing.
  • Fees are charged even if the application is denied.
  • Working as a licensed nursing professional without a license is a violation.

Exam

  • For the nursing knowledge exam, each section may be retaken only once. A final failure ends this path.
  • The practical skills exam requires a cooperating employer.

What happens if I only achieve a B1 on the telc exam?

How long does it take to receive my exam results?

The results of the telc German B1·B2 Nursing exam are available after four to six weeks.

Proficiency exam or adaptation course—which is better?

According to Sections 40 ff. of the Nursing Professions Act, the choice is yours. The proficiency exam is a single exam administered on one date, in which each section may be retaken only once. The adaptation course lasts for several months—about 6 months in Berlin, 8 to 11 months on average in Baden-Württemberg, and a maximum of 3 years—but it can be extended. There are no statistics on how long it takes to prepare for the proficiency exam.

How much more will I earn as a certified nursing professional?

According to the TVöD (collective bargaining agreement for public sector employees), an unskilled nursing assistant in pay grade P5 earns between 2,907 and 3,629 euros gross per month, while a certified nursing professional in pay grade P7 earns between 3,510 and 4,305 euros. The gross difference is around 600 to 760 euros; realistically, the net difference is 300 to 400 euros. Under the minimum wage for nursing care effective July 2026, unskilled nursing assistants will earn 16.52 euros per hour, while skilled nursing professionals will earn 21.03 euros.

Which language certificates are accepted?

In Bavaria, the following are accepted, among others: Goethe B2, Goethe-Test PRO Pflege, telc B2, telc Deutsch B1·B2 Pflege, TestDaF with TDN 3, ÖSD B2, ECL B2, the BAMF German Test for the Workplace B2, and the BSB’s nursing-specific language proficiency exam. telc certificates from Serbian and Bosnian institutions have not been accepted since June 23, 2022.

Is telc B2·C1 Medicine the same as telc B1·B2 Nursing?

No. telc German B2·C1 Medicine is the medical terminology exam and is not intended for nursing professionals. For nursing, the telc German B1·B2 Nursing is the appropriate exam.

Is the specialized language exam subsidized?

In Bremen, Hamburg, Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania, Schleswig-Holstein, Berlin, and Saxony-Anhalt, the specialized language exam costs 375 euros and is free if you are enrolled in a BAMF-funded B2 nursing course. The recognition grant, on the other hand, expressly does not cover language courses or language exam fees.

How old can my language certificate be for the visa?

For a visa under Section 16d of the Residence Act, the language certificate may not be more than 12 months old, and the provider must be ALTE-certified.

How do I prepare for the B2 exam while working my shifts?

The language course provides the structure; reviewing the material in between is what determines your score. A BAMF special course for healthcare professions comprises around 600 part-time class units—with days in between when your vocabulary and listening comprehension either grow or fade.
