How LinguaFlow Works

If you’re just memorizing vocabulary lists, you’re not learning a language. On this page, you’ll see firsthand how things feel different in just a few seconds: The sample text above is a real, interactive app view, and a native-speaker-quality voice will read this explanation to you right now while the words light up. With LinguaFlow, you learn languages the way your brain likes it: first understand, then listen, then speak. The method behind it is inspired by the Birkenbihl Method and consists of four steps: decoding, active listening, passive listening, and practice. Give it a try—tap a word above.

The Text View

At the top, you’ll see a text exactly as the app displays it. Each word is a small card: at the top, the word in your target language; below it, its meaning in your native language—word for word, in the order of the target language. Below that, the entire sentence is translated fluently. For languages with a different writing system, a line with the transliteration is added so you can read everything. Tap a word: The voice will continue playing from exactly that point.

Step 1: Decoding

This word-for-word translation is called decoding. It sounds intentionally clunky—because that’s exactly how you see how the language is really structured, without having to memorize grammar rules. In the past, people used to do this by hand for hours with a dictionary. In LinguaFlow, the app handles this step entirely for you—for any text you want to learn.

Step 2: Active Listening

Now you listen to the text while reading along. As a beginner, read the meaning line in your native language, listen to the target language at a slow pace, and press pause often—this helps your brain associate the sound with the meaning. As an advanced learner, read the original text and only glance down briefly when you encounter unfamiliar words. Make frequent use of A-B repetition: Select a single sentence and play it on a loop—try it out above.

Step 3: Passive Listening

Then let the text play in the background—while tidying up, going for a walk, or falling asleep. You don’t have to listen intently: Your brain will naturally absorb the sound and rhythm of the language. This is exactly how you prepare for speaking.

Step 4: Your Goal Determines the Next Steps

From here on, your learning goal determines the next steps. If you just want to understand the language, you’ve already reached your goal—just stick with steps two and three. If you want to speak, it’s time for choral speaking: Using the A-B repetition, pick a short sentence, set the speed to very slow, and speak along with the voice—just like in a choir. You’ll hear right away if something sounds different, and you’ll correct yourself automatically. You can do this anywhere, even on the tram: with headphones, it sounds like a phone call. And if you want to learn to write, just write down the text sentence by sentence.
